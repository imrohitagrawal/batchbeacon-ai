# BatchBeacon competitive positioning and broader product vision

Primary-source desk review,18October2026. This is a bounded review for the
submission, not an exhaustive market census or hands-on competitor benchmark.
Vendor pages describe advertised capabilities; they do not establish delivered
performance. No competitor account, paid trial or commercial contact was created.

## Investor and judge proposition

**BatchBeacon connects the product in someone's hands to official recall evidence
and an accountable response.** Retail investigations are the demonstrated starting
point. The proposed platform would extend that evidence into consumer discovery
and permissioned insights for producers and authorities across markets.

This is a coherent expansion thesis, not a claim that the current prototype
already has three public dashboards or worldwide coverage. Current retail staff
and managers use the workbench/owned queue; the operator has separate private
Operations. The Consumer Recall Explorer is planned. Authority/producer insights
require new data, permissions, review and validation.

## Three Opportunities answers

**How different is it?** Our product vision connects package-level retail
investigations, consumer discovery and permissioned producer/authority insights
around inspectable official evidence. The demonstrated distinction is our concrete
implementation of the evidence-to-response workflow: image proposals remain
separate from deterministic identity/batch checks, uncertainty and human action.
The combination is a positioning hypothesis; superiority and exclusivity remain
unproven.

**How does it solve the problem?** A retailer can connect a physical label to a
selected notice, review its conditions and retain the decision for handover.
The planned consumer view would show affected products/batches, notice dates,
reported hazards and official instructions. Planned producer/authority views
would help investigate repeated issues, source coverage and response gaps across
jurisdictions. Country, batch and time scope remain visible throughout.

**What is the USP?** A traceable package-to-response experience: AI helps read the
evidence; verifiable rules and accountable people own the response. Each saved
investigation retains the basis for review and follow-up. A matching lot on the
wrong product cannot replace identity verification; unresolved evidence remains
in review. This is demonstrable product value, not a claim that no other vendor
uses similar principles.

Start with a bounded retailer pilot, establish useful completion and evidence
quality, then add persistent accounts and reviewed source coverage. Expand consumer
discovery and permissioned analytics only after data rights, reliability and
access boundaries are validated. A retailer subscription or institutional service
is a future business-model hypothesis; no customer, revenue, pricing or willingness
to pay has been validated.

Pilot metrics: first-investigation completion/time, help needed, evidence
completeness, unsupported actions, recapture recovery, repeat use and cost per
completed investigation. Compare against manual lookup on the same reviewed cases.
Tutorial availability is evidence of onboarding functionality, not adoption uplift.

## Recall transparency is not a brand safety score

Raw recall counts can answer a narrow question: how many distinct published events
appear in the selected sources and time window. They cannot alone answer whether
one brand is less safe, more error-prone or more credible. Larger sales volumes,
more product lines, voluntary detection/reporting and different surveillance
coverage can change counts without establishing comparative risk. These are
analytical confounders to validate, not measured findings about particular brands.

The [FDA definitions](https://www.fda.gov/safety/enforcement-reports/enforcement-report-information-and-definitions)
distinguish events from products: one event can include several recalled products.
Classification and publication can follow a firm's earlier public warning.
Therefore row counts and report dates are not interchangeable with incident counts
and first-warning dates.

A credible planned analytics view needs:

1. **Provenance:** source, publication/update dates, reviewed version and coverage.
2. **Deduplication:** event versus product versus notice-update identities.
3. **Entity resolution:** brand, legal producer, private-label owner and importer
   kept distinct; aliases and disputed links reviewable.
4. **Context:** hazard/reason, authority-assigned severity, product category,
   affected quantity and distribution geography where documented.
5. **Denominators:** comparable sales/exposure and period if estimating rates.
   Missing denominators must remain unknown; counts must not masquerade as rates.
6. **Response evidence:** documented notice/acknowledgement/action dates and closure
   only from a source that supports them; distinguish self-reports from verification.
7. **Fair comparisons:** consistent source coverage, categories and definitions;
   uncertainty, missingness and correction history visible.
8. **Governance:** minimum aggregation, permissioned operational data, human review
   and correction/appeal process before any consequential published assessment.

Preferred product language: **Recall history and response transparency**, not
“unsafe brand leaderboard” or automatic credibility score. A regulator may use
reviewed patterns to prioritize investigation; the software must not assert a
violation or prescribe enforcement from counts alone.

## Cross-country feasibility and source limitations

A country-specific recall need not apply to every package sold elsewhere. Link
related notices while retaining jurisdiction, product identity, batch, dates,
market distribution and official instructions. No match in our sources is not
safety clearance. A reported hazard is not proof of a disease case or causation.

Public data is not uniformly complete. RASFF public summaries omit commercial
brand/operator details, so brand-level coverage cannot be promised from that
source alone. Its authority exchange and public summaries also have different
access boundaries. [European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/rasff_en).

The [openFDA food enforcement API](https://open.fda.gov/apis/food/enforcement/)
is updated weekly and explicitly cautions against using it for public alerts or
recall-lifecycle tracking. A future connector must use sources suitable for its
specific purpose, rather than relabelling historical enforcement data as a live
consumer alert feed. No new source connector is implemented by this research.

## What can be claimed now

The accepted release demonstrates actual Gemini through bounded Google ADK,
deterministic historical-SFA comparison, explicit simulated hold and persistent
owned queue. It includes image previews, plain-English explanations and an
optional five-step tour. Source/version checks, ownership, cost admission and
unknown-outcome recovery are supported by their separate engineering evidence.
Fixed guidance evaluation/activation/rollback is separately verified.

The current source scope is one reviewed historical SFA notice. UK remains
review-only. Consumer accounts/dashboard, notice/PDF image extraction, worldwide
matching, supplier scoring, regulatory analytics, full protected evaluations,
measured accuracy, adoption and commercial outcomes are not completed claims.

## Later market-entry extension: requirements, not only recalls

A producer considering a new country needs to distinguish recall history from
whether its formulation meets that destination's requirements. REG-01 proposes
product/formulation → destination/category/date → reviewed official rules →
possible restriction or missing information → specialist review. It is not an
implemented capability or a new current-release requirement.

The European Commission describes additive permissions together with conditions
of use, labelling and specifications, rather than an unconditional country-wide
allowed/banned flag. [EU rules](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/food-improvement-agents/additives/eu-rules_en).
[ePing](https://www.epingalert.org/) already supports discovery of product-requirement
notifications, so international requirements intelligence is also an existing
category. A notification must not be confused with an enacted rule in force.

Required future evidence includes ingredient identity, concentration, food category,
intended use and market/date. Missing formulation facts must remain unknown.
The output would cite possible restrictions and questions to resolve; it would
not certify that the product can be imported, sold or consumed. See
[REG-01](../ROADMAP.md#reg-01--destination-market-requirements-review) for bounded
future admission and evaluation dependencies.
